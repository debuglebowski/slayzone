# Meeting notes — Disaster Recovery Table Top Exercise

**Date:** _<date>_ · **Duration:** ~50 min · **Facilitator:** Kalle Hansson
**Attendees:** Max Netterberg, Philip Ngo, Dennis Landfors, Sara Landfors, Sahba Sadeghi

> Reconstructed from the recording and summarised into sections. Timestamps are relative to the start of the exercise (+mm:ss); short quotes are paraphrased from the discussion.

## Details

### Introduction and scenario overview (+0:00–3:00)
- Kalle Hansson welcomed the group and framed the session as a discussion-based exercise to validate the Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Plan, noting "this is a discussion, not a test — the goal is to find where we'd hesitate" (+0:35).
- He presented the scenario: at ~14:10 on a normal weekday, error rates spiked, the API began returning 500s, and the primary production database on Azure was unreachable with core tables missing; the customer-facing product was effectively down (+1:30).
- Kalle Hansson stated the objectives — validate the team's ability to recover, confirm the documented procedures are accurate, and surface anything that should be improved (+2:30).

### Detection and incident declaration (+3:00–7:00)
- Max Netterberg said the failure surfaced through monitoring/error-rate alerts, landing alongside the first customer reports (+3:25).
- As the first step, Sara Landfors created the **Security Event Report** and Philip Ngo opened the **Security Incident Response** (linked to the event ticket), kicking off the formal incident process (+4:00).
- Dennis Landfors took the incident-coordination role — keeping the team aligned and the CEO informed — so engineering could focus on the fix; the on-call engineer formally declared the incident (+4:40).
- Max Netterberg stressed the first technical move is to confirm scope before changing anything: which systems are affected, and whether the database is truly gone or just unreachable (+5:50).
- Max Netterberg added he would freeze deploys and changes to the environment immediately to avoid compounding the problem (+6:40).

### Diagnosis (+7:00–13:00)
- Max Netterberg observed that, with no security alerting triggered, the most probable cause was an erroneous change run against production rather than a malicious act — "this looks like a bad migration, not an attacker" (+8:10).
- Dennis Landfors noted the immediate response is identical either way — stabilise, restore, verify — and that it would be reclassified as a security breach only if evidence of compromise appeared (+9:45).
- Philip Ngo added that production access should be locked down during recovery to prevent further accidental writes, and that the offending change should be identified so it isn't repeated on restore (+11:20).

### System recovery and backup restoration (DR) (+13:00–26:00)
- Max Netterberg walked through the restore: locate the most recent automated Azure backup — completed at ~02:00, roughly 12 hours earlier — and restore to a **fresh** database instance rather than overwriting the damaged one, preserving the original for investigation (+14:20).
- Philip Ngo described the cutover: once the restored instance is healthy, repoint the application/API connection to it and bring services back gradually (+16:35).
- Philip Ngo detailed integrity verification before cutover — row/record counts against expected ranges, key foreign-key checks, and an application smoke test of critical user flows (+18:15).
- Max Netterberg estimated full restore plus verification at ~3–4 hours, comfortably inside the **12-hour RTO** defined in Appendix B (+20:30).
- Sahba Sadeghi asked, in plain terms, whether this meant losing the last several hours of data; the group confirmed the ~12 hours between the 02:00 backup and the incident would be lost, which sits within the **24-hour RPO** and is accepted (+22:10).
- Max Netterberg noted more frequent backups would shrink that worst-case loss; Kalle Hansson acknowledged it as primarily a cost trade-off and proposed revisiting backup frequency as revenue grows rather than committing now (+24:05).
- Max Netterberg confirmed the backup-restoration step would be captured separately as evidence (a real test restore), satisfying the plan's annual requirement (+25:30).

### Customer impact and communications (BC) (+26:00–34:00)
- Kalle Hansson raised an inbound message from an enterprise customer asking whether their data was gone and whether this was a breach (+27:40).
- Sara Landfors confirmed it is not a breach and that she and Customer Support own all external messaging (+29:20).
- Sara Landfors said the message should be honest about impact, contain no PII, and give an ETA only once the restore is confirmed underway — "we don't promise a time until we're actually restoring" (+31:10).
- Sara Landfors added the team would send proactive notice via email and the status page rather than wait for inbound questions, and would follow up with an all-clear once resolved (+33:00).

### Continuity while degraded (+34:00–40:00)
- Dennis Landfors said work during the outage would be prioritised strictly by customer impact, with non-critical work paused until service is restored (+35:15).
- Dennis Landfors added that internal updates would be given at regular intervals until the all-clear so everyone has a current picture (+37:00).
- Sara Landfors confirmed decision authority rests with the incident coordinator for recovery, with her sign-off for customer-impacting decisions such as service credits or extended-outage messaging (+38:45).

### Review and action items (+40:00–50:00)
- Dennis Landfors summarised that the plan procedures were broadly accurate and recovery was achievable within the documented targets (+41:30).
- Kalle Hansson noted that, while the plan exists for good reason, several steps add overhead in a live incident; the process should be simplified — with a lightweight "first-response quick-card" — so bureaucracy never stands between the team and fixing the issue (+44:10).
- The group discussed the exercise itself and agreed it was realistic and useful (+46:20).
- Three actions were agreed (+48:30):
  1. Simplify the BC/DR plan and add a first-response quick-card — **Kalle Hansson**.
  2. Create a pre-approved customer outage-comms template — **Sara Landfors**.
  3. Revisit backup frequency as budget allows during the year — **Kalle Hansson**.
