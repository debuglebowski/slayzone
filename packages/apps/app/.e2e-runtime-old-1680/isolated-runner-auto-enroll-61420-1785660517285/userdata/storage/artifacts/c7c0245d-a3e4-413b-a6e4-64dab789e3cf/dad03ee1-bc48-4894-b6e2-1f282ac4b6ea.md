# UC07 — M&A Data-Room Deep Dive

**Persona**: M&A counsel, investment committee chair, or VP of corporate development.

**Scenario**: A target company's full virtual data room — 100+ documents including the latest 10-K and 10-Qs, all material contracts (customer, supplier, license, employment, leases), 8-K filings, IP filings, regulatory correspondence, board minutes summaries, audit letters. The reviewer has 10 deep questions that each require synthesis across the WHOLE room, not per-doc extraction.

**The 10 questions are deep, not shallow**. Examples:
- What are the top 5 risks not yet disclosed in the most recent 10-K?
- Where are hidden change-of-control trip-wires across the contract portfolio?
- What's the company's actual customer concentration vs what's disclosed?
- Which key employees have IP-assignment gaps that survive their departure?
- What MAC triggers exist across contracts that an acquirer would inherit?
- Is there off-balance-sheet exposure suggested by footnotes + lease agreements + JV docs?
- What litigation patterns emerge from 10-K disclosures + 8-K filings + audit letters?
- Where do management discussion claims contradict primary contract terms?
- What regulatory exposure exists that hasn't been flagged in risk factors?
- What's the realistic worst-case net-working-capital adjustment based on the contract bundle?

**Question types**:
- Synthesis across many docs (the hard part)
- Risk identification requiring inference, not extraction
- Pattern detection across heterogeneous formats
- Gap identification (what's missing or implied but unstated)

**Sample docs (~100+)**: Pulled from SEC EDGAR for one or two public target companies. Real M&A data rooms aren't public, but a public company's full SEC filing trail (10-K, 10-Qs, 8-Ks, material contracts exhibits, proxy statements, registration statements) gives a faithful proxy at scale.

**What this tests**: Reasoning depth × corpus breadth. This is the test where Normain's "verifiable insights from complex documents" wedge plays hardest — competitors will either run out of context window, drift on citations, or fall victim to lost-in-the-middle. The use case where Normain's win margin should be largest.
